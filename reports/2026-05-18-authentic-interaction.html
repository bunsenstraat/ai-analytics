Key takeaways

  • Busiest day in dataset (922 users, 2,049 prompts) but authentic engagement rate actually dropped to 25.8% — the volume spike was driven by the Chinese homework cohort and canned button usage, not by organic explorers.
  • The Chinese university cohort is the most significant new finding. These are likely students completing assignments, not independent developers. They're real users getting value, but they're not a monetization target — they're probably using a free tier indefinitely and their engagement is episodic (assignment deadlines).
  • Canned prompt distribution is remarkably stable across all three days: vulnerability check ~39%, explain error ~27%, explain code ~18%. These three buttons account for 85%+ of all canned usage every day.
  • The power user tail is deeper on May 18 — top 10 users averaged 21.7 manual prompts each vs. ~17 on May 26/30. More heavy users on a busier day.
  • Top 10% concentration dropped to 41.7% (vs. ~48% on the other days) — slightly more evenly distributed engagement, possibly due to the homework cohort spreading prompts across more users.
  • Russian language appeared as a second non-English segment alongside Chinese, suggesting Remix is reaching audiences well beyond its English-speaking core.
